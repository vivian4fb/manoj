Method

Why the colours never move

Four hues carry four roles, and they carry them everywhere: in the films, on the slides, in the figures on the lesson page, and in the field at the top of this one. An amber arrow always means the same quantity.

  1. 01

    Nothing is typed in. Every figure in every scene is computed from the vector components and passed through a guard against a hand-worked value. A drift of any size aborts the render rather than shipping a wrong number.

  2. 02

    The palette defers to the films. Eleven films are already rendered, so the page takes its colours from them and not the other way round. The design search proposed an education teal as a brand colour; it collides with the hue that already means “third vector”, so it was refused.

  3. 03

    Register over friendliness. The same search proposed Baloo 2 and Comic Neue for “education”. Those are tuned for children’s apps. The reader here is seventeen and sitting an A level, so the page is set in IBM Plex — condensed for headings, serif for prose, mono for every number.

  4. 04

    The field is real. The arrows above are a divergence-free vector field sampled on a lattice, coloured by which role each arrow is playing. It is not a particle effect, and if your machine cannot run it the panel falls back to the drawn triangle with nothing lost.
