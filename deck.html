Scalar or vector?

1.4.1
  • A scalar is finished at a number and a unit. A vector is not stated until you give a direction.
  • The test: reverse it. If something changed, it was a vector.
  • Once round a 400 m track — distance 400 m, displacement 0.
In the exam. Momentum and weight are vectors because each is built from one: mass × velocity, mass × g.

Drawing a vector

1.4
  • Length is the magnitude, read against a scale you state: 1 cm ≡ 2.0 N.
  • The arrowhead is the direction, measured from a reference you also state.
  • Write it F⃗, F or F underlined; the magnitude alone is |F⃗|.
In the exam. An angle quoted with no reference earns nothing. "35° above the horizontal" — or a three-figure bearing.

Tip to tail

1.4.2
  • Slide the second vector until its tail meets the first tip — without turning it.
  • The resultant runs first tail to last tip.
  • a + b = b + a. Draw both orders and you have the parallelogram.
In the exam. On a scale drawing, state the scale before you measure anything off it.

04

At right angles

1.4.2
  • No instruments needed: R = √(Fx² + Fy²).
  • θ = tan⁻¹(Fy / Fx), measured from the Fx direction.
  • Learn 3–4–5 and 5–12–13 — examiners use them so arithmetic isn't the test.
In the exam. "53°" is not an answer. "53° above east", or "bearing 037°", is.

05

Not at right angles

1.4.2
  • Same triangle, harder trigonometry. A careful scale drawing is still accepted.
  • R² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C, where C is the triangle's interior angle.
  • If the angle between them is φ, then C = 180° − φ.
In the exam. cos 130° is negative — that is exactly why the resultant beats both forces.

06

Subtracting

1.4.2
  • There is no separate subtraction rule: a − b = a + (−b).
  • Reversing a vector changes its direction and nothing else.
  • b − a is the same length as a − b, pointing the opposite way.
In the exam. This is the whole method behind every change-in-velocity and change-in-momentum question.

A bounce changes velocity, not speed

1.4.2 · 3.3
2mv

the momentum change at a straight bounce — not zero, and not mv

  • Δv = vfinal − vinitial — a vector subtraction.
  • Choose a positive direction, state it, then it is only signs.
In the exam. (−5.0) − (+5.0) = −10 m s⁻¹. Writing 5.0 − 5.0 = 0 is the single most common error in this topic.

Resolving

1.4.3
  • Addition run backwards: any vector splits into two perpendicular parts that rebuild it exactly.
  • Along the direction: F cos θ. At right angles to it: F sin θ.
  • θ is measured from the "along" direction — not from the horizontal by default.
In the exam. Test it by letting θ → 0: the along-component should become the whole force.

09

Weight on a slope

1.4.3
  • Nothing says the axes must be horizontal and vertical. Choose them along and into the slope.
  • Down the slope: W sin θ — the part that accelerates it.
  • Into the slope: W cos θ — the part the surface pushes back.
In the exam. Sanity-check at θ = 0°: a flat surface carries the whole weight, and nothing slides.

Equilibrium closes the triangle

4.2.3
  • Zero resultant means the last arrowhead lands on the first tail. The triangle closes.
  • ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0 say the same thing in algebra.
  • The balancing force is equal and opposite to the resultant of the other two.
In the exam. Draw them in order, each tail on the previous tip. Out of order, it will not close.

11

Crossing a river

1.4 applied
  • The most useful consequence of resolving: perpendicular components do not interfere.
  • t = width / vboat — the current cannot delay the crossing.
  • drift = vcurrent × t — it only decides where you land.
In the exam. The same independence is what makes projectile motion solvable: horizontal and vertical, separately.

The whole topic, in five lines

magnitude · perpendicular

R = √(Fx² + Fy²)

direction · perpendicular

θ = tan⁻¹(Fy / Fx)

magnitude · any angle

R² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

components

F cos θ along · F sin θ at 90°

equilibrium

ΣFx = 0 · ΣFy = 0 — equivalently, a closed vector triangle

14

Three marks people throw away

01 · direction

A magnitude with no direction

Half the marks in a resultant question are for the direction, and for the reference it is measured from.

02 · signs

Δv = 0 at a bounce

The two velocities are in opposite directions. One of them is negative, so the change is 2v, not zero.

03 · trigonometry

sin and cos swapped

Cosine belongs to the direction the angle is measured from. Check the limit at 0° before you commit.
